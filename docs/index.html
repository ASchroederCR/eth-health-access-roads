How the score works

Tier = rain (0–3) + surface (paved 0, unpaved 1, +1 if mapped in bad condition) + steepness (0–2) − 1, kept to 0–3 and at most one tier above the rain score. No forecast rain means Normal. Speed losses (Minor ~10%, Elevated ~25%, Severe ~50%) are illustrative assumptions, not measured.

Rain is judged two ways, each for days 1–3, 4–7 and 8–15. Accumulation drives the slowdown tiers: the wettest 10-day running total in the window, counting rain PARDIG observed before the forecast plus WeatherNext 3's bias-corrected forecast (low ≥ 25 mm, moderate ≥ 50 mm, high ≥ 100 mm). Intense rain marks where a downpour is possible: the wettest hour of WeatherNext 3's hourly 90th percentile, with thresholds set so it flags as often as PARDIG observed ≥ 10 mm (or ≥ 20 mm) in an hour in early November 2026. The forecast's hourly rain runs lower than observed, so those thresholds are only about 3–7 mm in the forecast's wettest hour; a hospital's chart can peak well under 10 mm in an hour where intense rain is flagged. "All 15 days" shows the worst window. Health-facility travel times follow the selected window: each window is routed with its own road tiers, and "All 15 days" uses each road's worst tier. Forecast skill falls after about day 7, so days 8–15 are lower confidence.

Slip / rock-fall exposure needs intense rain or high accumulation on ground ≥ 26.6° that NASA rates moderately or more landslide-prone, or very high susceptibility on ground ≥ 14°. Rain amounts shown use a year-round monthly bias correction; hazard classes use a stronger correction that caught the most observed hazard in tests.

Health access: two trips are timed separately. Every health centre and health post is timed to its nearest hospital; health posts are also timed to their nearest health centre. The map colours one trip at a time (choose under Health facility access). Each trip is the shortest road travel time at normal speeds by road class (trunk 70 … track 15 km/h, unpaved × 0.7, track grade 5 or sand / mud a further × 0.6), then to that same facility with each road slowed by its tier. A hospital's linked facilities are the health centres and posts for which it is the nearest hospital by road; this is a road-network grouping, not a population catchment or the official referral pathway. Facilities are approved hospitals, health centres and health posts from the Ethiopian health facility register.

Travel model: the road + slope model routes over the mapped road network with class speeds and walking to the road. The MAP friction surface (Malaria Atlas Project 2019 motorized, ~1 km) routes cell to cell using MAP's road, land-cover and off-road speeds, with cells crossed by a scored road slowed by the same tier factors. Both use the same facilities and the same nearest-hospital rule. MAP's journeys come out about 3–4 times shorter, so the two models usually agree on which facilities are worst hit but not on how many minutes are added.

Flood exposure (first-guess thresholds, not yet checked against observed road floods; not used in travel times): a ~110 m piece of road is low-lying if it is no more than 3 m above the nearest drainage with a river draining 100 km² or more within 1 km (MERIT Hydro), or on ground that satellites saw under water at least 10% of the time (JRC, 1984–2021) and no more than 5 m above drainage; it is flagged when the window's 10-day running rain reaches 50 mm or very intense rain is possible. River crossings come from OpenStreetMap waterways and, where OSM maps none, MERIT Hydro channels draining 50 km² or more. On rivers GloFAS resolves (about 250 km² and up), a crossing is shown when at least 30% of the GloFAS 51-member river-flow forecast exceeds the 2-, 5- or 20-year flood on any day of the window, which counts rain falling anywhere upstream: fords, crossings with no bridge mapped and culverts from the 2-year level; bridges, including those assumed on trunk, primary and secondary roads, only at the 20-year level. On smaller streams only fords and unbridged crossings are shown, and only where very intense rain is possible and the 10-day rain reaches 100 mm (flash floods).

Slip / rock-fall exposure is judged on each ~110 m piece of road: moderate or high forecast rain over that piece, and either ground ≥ 26.6° where NASA's global landslide susceptibility map rates it moderate or higher, or very high susceptibility on ground ≥ 14°. Only the exposed stretches are drawn and counted.

Surface is mapped (OSM tag or Mapillary image model) for about a fifth of road length; the rest is inferred from road class. The road network is the current OpenStreetMap extract (Geofabrik); roads tagged under construction, no access or impassable are left out. Road grades come from a ~110 m elevation grid and run high where roads cut across hillsides.
