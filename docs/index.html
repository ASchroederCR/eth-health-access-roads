How the score works

Tier = rain (0–3) + surface (paved 0, unpaved 1, +1 if mapped in bad condition) + steepness (0–2) − 1, kept to 0–3 and at most one tier above the rain score. No forecast rain means Normal. Speed losses (Minor ~10%, Elevated ~25%, Severe ~50%) are illustrative assumptions, not measured.

Rain is judged two ways, each for days 1–3, 4–7 and 8–15. Accumulation drives the slowdown tiers: the wettest 10-day running total in the window, counting rain PARDIG observed before the forecast plus WeatherNext 3's bias-corrected forecast (low ≥ 25 mm, moderate ≥ 50 mm, high ≥ 100 mm). Intense rain marks where a downpour is possible: the wettest hour of WeatherNext 3's hourly 90th percentile, with thresholds set so it flags as often as PARDIG observed ≥ 10 mm (or ≥ 20 mm) in an hour in early November 2026. "All 15 days" shows the worst window; travel times and health-facility results use it. Forecast skill falls after about day 7, so days 8–15 are lower confidence.

Slip / rock-fall exposure needs intense rain or high accumulation on ground ≥ 26.6° that NASA rates moderately or more landslide-prone, or very high susceptibility on ground ≥ 14°. Rain amounts shown use a year-round monthly bias correction; hazard classes use a stronger correction that caught the most observed hazard in tests.

Health access: shortest road travel time from each facility to the nearest hospital (health posts: nearest health centre) at normal speeds by road class (trunk 70 … track 15 km/h, unpaved × 0.7), then to that same facility with each road slowed by its tier. A hospital's linked facilities are the health centres and posts for which it is the nearest hospital by road; this is a road-network grouping, not a population catchment or the official referral pathway. Facilities are approved hospitals, health centres and health posts from the Ethiopian health facility register.

Travel model: the road + slope model routes over the mapped road network with class speeds and walking to the road. The MAP friction surface (Malaria Atlas Project 2019 motorized, ~1 km) routes cell to cell using MAP's road, land-cover and off-road speeds, with cells crossed by a scored road slowed by the same tier factors. Both use the same facilities and the same nearest-hospital rule. MAP's journeys come out about 3–4 times shorter, so the two models usually agree on which facilities are worst hit but not on how many minutes are added.

Slip / rock-fall exposure is judged on each ~110 m piece of road: moderate or high forecast rain over that piece, and either ground ≥ 26.6° where NASA's global landslide susceptibility map rates it moderate or higher, or very high susceptibility on ground ≥ 14°. Only the exposed stretches are drawn and counted.

Surface is mapped (OSM tag or Mapillary image model) for about a quarter of road length; the rest is inferred from road class. Road grades come from a ~110 m elevation grid and run high where roads cut across hillsides.
